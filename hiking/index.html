Hiking

Some of the hikes I've done over the years.

Highlights

  • Summited Mount Kilimanjaro via the Lemosho route, 7 days to reach 19,341 feet, the highest point on the African continent. An incredible experience!
  • A multi-week east-to-west coast road trip with my sister through national parks across the northern US, including Maquoketa Caves, Badlands, Grand Teton, Yellowstone, Glacier, and Mount Rainier, before finishing with hiking in British Columbia and driving back through Canada.
  • Watched the sunrise from Pico do Areeiro in Madeira, then hiked PR1 along the ridge to Pico Ruivo, the highest peak on the island, including the trail's "Stairway to Heaven" section.
  • Working towards completing all 46 Adirondack High Peaks (the "46ers"), the 46 mountains in the Adirondacks over 4,000 feet.

Pico Grande via Caminho Real da Encumeada (PR12)

September 16, 2026 · Madeira, Portugal

PR12 climbs to Pico Grande, starting from the Boca da Corrida viewpoint. Getting to the trailhead meant a steep, narrow drive up, and I started hiking around 7:30 AM as the sun was coming up. Compared to the other trails I did on this trip, it was noticeably quieter and less touristy, which I liked.

The first 3.5 km or so is mostly rolling terrain, up and down without much net elevation change, before the final 1.5 km turns into a steep, direct climb to the summit. I had the top completely to myself, with 360-degree panoramic views out over the surrounding mountains. One of the best summits of the trip.

Ponta de São Lourenço Trail (PR8)

September 15, 2026 · Madeira, Portugal

PR8 runs out to the easternmost tip of Madeira and is probably the island's second most popular hike after PR1, so it was busy the whole way. Registration slots were booked up, and the only time I could get was a 4 PM start. The terrain here is completely different from the rest of the island: volcanic, dry, and barren, with basalt and tuff underfoot and no shade or tree cover along the entire route.

It was hot, though not unbearable until the very end, where the trail climbs steeply up toward the tip. The views made up for it, cliffs dropping straight into the ocean on a narrow peninsula that felt like a completely different island from the lush hikes I'd done elsewhere on Madeira.

Porto da Cruz - Boca do Risco via Vereda do Larano

September 14, 2026 · Madeira, Portugal

An out-and-back coastal cliff walk along the Vereda do Larano to Boca do Risco, starting and ending in the town of Porto da Cruz. I parked in the town centre and walked up the steep roads to the trailhead while it was still dark, so the sun only started coming up once I was on the trail. The path itself is narrow and runs right along the seaside cliffs.

I had lunch at the turnaround point at Boca do Risco, with a view stretching down the whole coastline, before heading back the way I came. Once I was back in town, I stopped into a small surf bar to watch the surfers. Porto da Cruz is known for having good waves, so there's usually a crowd out on the water.

I finished the day at Engenhos do Norte, a rum distillery just outside town. The current building dates to 1927 and still runs on 19th-century steam-powered machinery, one of the last traditional distilleries of its kind left in Europe. I tried a few of their rums before heading out.

Achadas da Cruz Cable Car via Vereda do Calhau

September 13, 2026 · Madeira, Portugal

The Achadas da Cruz cable car is billed as the steepest in Europe, with a 98% grade at the top and a drop of 451 metres over a 600-metre run. Looking down the cliff from the top station was enough to make me a little nervous before getting in the car. It runs as two four-person cars sharing a cable, one going down as the other comes up, and the ride takes about five minutes.

At the bottom there's a small farming and fishing community and a promenade running past a handful of farms. Before the cable car was built, the only way in or out was on foot via the Vereda do Calhau, the same trail people still use to move produce down from the farms up top, since crops apparently grow better higher up the mountain. That trail is what I hiked back up.

It's short but almost entirely straight uphill, and it was hot enough that I was pretty worn out by the top. Climbing it also meant I could see how far up the cliff I'd gotten the whole way up.

Fanal Forest

September 13, 2026 · Madeira, Portugal

More of a walk than a hike. Fanal sits on the Paul da Serra plateau at around 1,150 metres and is part of Madeira's Laurisilva, a UNESCO World Heritage forest that's the largest surviving stretch of primary laurel forest in the world. The til trees, some estimated to be over 800 years old, are gnarled and moss-covered with branches that twist down to the ground and back up again.

It looked incredible.

Pico do Areeiro to Pico Ruivo (PR1)

September 12, 2026 · Madeira, Portugal

PR1 (Vereda do Areeiro) connects Pico do Areeiro (5,965 ft / 1,818 m) to Pico Ruivo (6,109 ft / 1,862 m), the highest peak on Madeira, finishing at the Achada do Teixeira Mountain Spot Lodge. It's the most popular and most photographed hike on the island, famous for its "Stairway to Heaven" section. Registration passes for the trail were sold out by the time I planned my trip, so I booked it through a local guide company instead.

On my second full day in Madeira, I got picked up at 5:30 AM and taken by shuttle with a small group to the trailhead, where we watched the sunrise alongside a crowd of other hikers before starting out. From there it was completely unguided: the company just handled the drive and a quick briefing, then left us to hike the trail ourselves at our own pace, as long as we were back by a set time.

The hike itself was beautiful, and the light kept improving as the sun rose higher and lit up the mountains around me. I stopped at one of the peaks for a while to just take in the view, since I had some time to spare before I needed to be back at the meeting point. One of the best experiences of the trip.

Lower Great Range Traverse

August 15, 2026 · Adirondacks, New York, USA

I did the Lower Great Range Traverse with Justin. We got to the Marcy Fields parking lot a little after 7 AM and took the shuttle to the Garden trailhead. From there, we hiked along Johns Brook and then climbed the Ore Bed Brook Trail, which had a lot of steep steps. Once we reached the ridge, we headed north and took the "cable" route, a series of chains anchored into the side of the mountain, up toward the peaks.

We first stopped at a false summit before taking the out-and-back detour to Pyramid Peak (4,515 ft). Pyramid had the nicest view of the day. It was sunny and warm on that side of the ridge, with very little wind. From there, we continued across Gothics Mountain (4,736 ft), Armstrong Mountain (4,400 ft), Upper Wolfjaw Mountain (4,185 ft), and Lower Wolfjaw Mountain (4,173 ft).

After Lower Wolfjaw, we descended the Wolfjaw Trail back to Johns Brook and followed it out to the Garden trailhead before returning to Marcy Fields. Overall, the hike was about 27 kilometres with 1,700 metres of elevation gain. Gothics, Armstrong, Upper Wolfjaw, and Lower Wolfjaw were #19 through #22 on my journey toward becoming an Adirondack 46er.

Upper Great Range Traverse

July 25–26, 2026 · Adirondacks, New York, USA

The Upper Great Range Traverse crosses some of the most rugged terrain in the Adirondack High Peaks. We climbed Saddleback Mountain (4,515 ft), Basin Mountain (4,827 ft), Little Haystack, Mount Haystack (4,960 ft), and Mount Marcy (5,344 ft), in that order. Saddleback, Basin, Haystack, and Marcy were #15 through #18 on my journey toward becoming an Adirondack 46er.

We arrived at the Garden trailhead a little after 7 AM, but the parking lot was already full, so we parked at Marcy Fields and took the shuttle back to the trailhead. We planned to camp overnight and had heavy packs carrying all of our camping gear. After hiking about seven kilometres past Johns Brook Lodge and up Ore Bed Brook, we reached a wild camping spot, set up our tents, dropped off the heavy gear, and continued toward Saddleback with our day packs.

It was a hot, sunny day. Once we reached the ridge, the route involved a lot of quick elevation loss and regain as we moved from peak to peak, which was exhausting. The views were great, and Haystack was my favourite summit, with a 360-degree view of both the Great Range and Marcy. Marcy also had a good view, but as the highest peak in New York, it was a little crowded. We still found room for a quick nap at the summit before carrying on.

We reached our campsite around 8 PM, just as it was getting dark. We made some food and looked at the stars, which are incredible in the Adirondacks, before going to bed. We hiked out the next morning, stopped at the farmers market at Marcy Fields after getting back to the car, and finished the trip with a swim in the Ausable River.

Mount Mansfield

July 19, 2026 · Green Mountains, Vermont, USA

Fourth time hiking Mount Mansfield. It is the highest peak in Vermont, reaching 4,393 feet (1,339 meters). The summit ridge is known for its distinctive profile, which looks like a human face when viewed from the east, and the upper mountain is home to rare alpine tundra.

I did this hike with Alex. We arrived at the trailhead around 6 AM, and the fog made the walk through the woods feel a little eerie. By the time we reached the treeline, it was even foggier and incredibly windy.

There were basically no views along the ridge. We could barely see 30 feet in front of us, and the wind was forcing the fog straight across the mountain. It was actually really cool and made the hike feel completely different from my previous trips up Mansfield. We had a beer at the summit while looking at absolutely nothing, but luckily, as we started heading down, we dropped below the clouds and got some nice sunny views across Vermont.

Whiteface, Lookout, and Esther Mountains

July 6, 2026 · Adirondacks, New York, USA

Whiteface Mountain (4,867 ft) and Esther Mountain (4,240 ft) are both Adirondack 46ers. Whiteface is the fifth-highest peak in New York and is unusual among the High Peaks because there is an observatory and a road to the summit. Esther is a quieter side trip from the Wilmington Trail and does not have the same kind of open summit views.

This hike marked #13 and #14 on my journey toward becoming an Adirondack 46er: Esther was #13, and Whiteface was #14.

I got to the trailhead around 5:45 AM and was the first one there, so I had the entire trail to myself. It was still a little cool when I started, but it really started to heat up as the morning went on. I went out to Esther first, which did not have much in the way of views, and then continued up toward Whiteface.

The climb to Whiteface was the best part of the day. I had views of the mountain on the way up that I remembered from skiing at Whiteface a couple of years ago, when it was around -40 degrees (pictures from that at the end). I reached the summit a little before 9 AM, before the road and observatory opened, so I had the top to myself for a little while too. The views from the summit were really great: sunny, a little hazy, and a pretty perfect reward for the early start.

Dix Range

June 13, 2026 · Adirondacks, New York, USA

The Dix Range is a ridge of five Adirondack 46ers deep in the High Peaks Wilderness. It was a beautiful day, I arrived around 6:30 in the morning to find the main parking lot already full, so I had to park at the overflow lot and walk an extra 5km just to reach the trailhead.

I did the loop clockwise, heading up the Beckhorn Trail to the Beckhorn (4,485 ft), a sharp shoulder on the southern ridge of Dix, before continuing out to the summit of Dix Mountain (4,857 ft). From there I traversed south across the range to Hough Peak (4,400 ft), then South Dix (4,060 ft), then an out-and-back to Grace Peak (3,994 ft), and finally Macomb Mountain (4,405 ft). The descent was down the Macomb Slide, a steep and exposed landslide chute that was pretty sketchy in places.

All told it was about a 35km day with nearly 1,900 meters of elevation gain. Dix, Hough, South Dix, Grace, and Macomb are all Adirondack 46ers, bringing my total to #8 through #12.

Cascade Mountain and Porter Mountain

January 11, 2026 · Adirondacks, New York, USA

Cascade Mountain (4,098 ft) and Porter Mountain (4,059 ft) are two of the Adirondack 46ers, the 46 highest peaks in New York’s Adirondack Park. Cascade is one of the most popular of the 46ers due to its relatively short and accessible trail, while Porter is often done as a side trip from Cascade, adding a bit more distance to the hike.

This hike marked #4 and #5 on my journey toward becoming an Adirondack 46er. It was also my first winter hike of the year, and my first hike where I would be using microspikes and snowshoes, so we wanted to do a relatively easy hike to test out all the equipment and get used to it.

The microspikes were absolutely amazing. We were able to walk straight up large sheets of ice that had formed on the steep trail without slipping at all. There had been some recent rain that froze over, and there is no way we would have been able to make it up the mountain without them. We only really used our snowshoes once we got over to Porter Peak, which was less well traveled and had deeper, less compact snow, but it was still a lot of fun to finally get to use them.

Mount Katahdin

July 14, 2025 · Appalachian Mountains, Maine, USA

The second time I've done this mountain. Mount Katahdin is the highest peak in Maine, standing at 5,269 feet (1,606 meters). It's the northern end of the Appalachian Trail and sits in Baxter State Park. The mountain is known for its rugged ridges, steep climbs, and unpredictable weather.

The Knife Edge trail, which is the trail I took this time, is probably the most famous route up, with some exposed sections that are a bit sketchy if you're not a fan of heights. The summit is usually windy and can be cold even in the summer. Katahdin is also a big deal for thru-hikers finishing (or starting) the Appalachian Trail.

Mount Kilimanjaro

February 13, 2025 · Tanzania, Africa

Mount Kilimanjaro is the highest mountain in Africa, standing at 19,341 feet (5,895 meters). It's a massive stratovolcano with three main cones: Kibo, Mawenzi, and Shira. The summit, Uhuru Peak, is on Kibo and is the highest point on the continent.

The hike is long and the altitude makes it tough, especially near the top. You go through rainforest, moorland, alpine desert, and finally a rocky, icy summit, with glaciers near the top.

We did the Lemosho route up over 7 days, and it was an incredible experience.

Algonquin, Iroquois, and Wright Peaks

July 27, 2024 · Adirondacks, New York, USA

This hike covers three of the Adirondack High Peaks: Algonquin Peak (5,114 ft), which is the second highest mountain in New York, as well as Iroquois Peak (4,840 ft) and Wright Peak (4,587 ft). The route is steep and rocky, with a lot of scrambling and some exposed sections above the treeline. Weather can change quickly on these summits, and the wind at the top is no joke.

This hike marked #1, #2, and #3 on my journey toward becoming an Adirondack 46er. Algonquin is known for its open summit and wide views over the High Peaks Wilderness. The trail is popular but still feels wild, and you get a good mix of forest, bog bridges, and bare rock. Wright Peak is also known for the wreckage of a B-47 bomber that crashed near the summit in 1962, and you can still find some debris if you look around.

Mount Rainier

September 1, 2023 · Cascade Range, Washington, USA

Mount Rainier stands at 14,411 feet (4,392 meters), making it the highest peak in Washington and the Cascade Range. It's a massive stratovolcano, visible from miles around, and is known for its year-round glaciers and unpredictable weather. The mountain is surrounded by rugged terrain, alpine meadows, and dense forests.

We hiked up to Camp Muir, which is the highest you can go on the mountain without needing professional mountaineering gear like ropes or crampons. On the way up, we were stuck in a cloud and couldn't even see the direction we were heading—just following the route on AllTrails. We were walking on ice and snow most of the way, but when we got to the camp, we broke above the clouds and the view was incredible. This was my favourite hike on our roadtrip.

Glacier National Park

August 29, 2023 · Rocky Mountains, Montana, USA

Glacier National Park, located in the Rocky Mountains of northern Montana, is known for its peaks, alpine lakes, and over two dozen glaciers. The park is home to wildlife such as mountain goats, grizzly bears, and moose.

We hiked up to a historic mining tunnel that was dug into the mountain, which lets you walk all the way through to the other side.

Yellowstone National Park

August 26, 2023 · Wyoming/Montana/Idaho, USA

Yellowstone National Park is the first national park in the world, established in 1872, and spans across Wyoming, Montana, and Idaho. It's known for its geothermal features, including the Old Faithful geyser, as well as its canyons, alpine rivers, forests, and wildlife such as bison, elk, bears, and wolves.

Grand Teton National Park

August 25, 2023 · Teton Range, Wyoming, USA

Grand Teton National Park is located in northwestern Wyoming and known for its mountain scenery, lakes, and wildlife. The park's centerpiece is the Teton Range, with Grand Teton itself rising to 13,775 feet (4,199 meters), the highest peak in the range.

The park has hiking trails ranging from easy lakeside walks to challenging alpine routes, with views of rugged peaks, glacial valleys, and wildflower meadows along the way. It's also home to moose, elk, bears, and bald eagles.

At the midway point of our hike, my sister and I took a dip in a freezing cold pond and enjoyed a couple of drinks, which was a fantastic and memorable break during the trek.

Badlands National Park

August 22, 2023 · South Dakota, USA

Badlands National Park in South Dakota is known for its sharply eroded buttes, pinnacles, and spires, mixed with the largest undisturbed mixed grass prairie in the United States. The park covers nearly 244,000 acres and has hiking trails that wind through layered rock formations and fossil beds, including fossils of ancient mammals like the saber-toothed cat. The park is also home to bison, bighorn sheep, prairie dogs, and a wide variety of birds.

This was a two-day hike that my sister and I did together. It was incredibly hot and sunny during our trek, and after camping overnight, we ran out of water by the next morning—which was a bit concerning, but we made it back to the car safely. During the night, we also heard dogs howling in the distance, adding to the adventure.

Maquoketa Caves

August 20, 2023 · Iowa, USA

Maquoketa Caves State Park in eastern Iowa is known for its network of caves, limestone formations, and wooded trails. The park has more caves than any other state park in Iowa, including Dancehall Cave, which stretches over 1,000 feet, and several smaller caves accessible along the hiking paths.

This is the first park my sister and I stopped at on our roadtrip across the United States, starting from New Brunswick on the east coast.

Gros Morne

August 7, 2023 · Long Range Mountains, Newfoundland, Canada

Gros Morne Mountain is the second highest peak in Newfoundland, rising to 2,644 feet (806 meters) above sea level. It's located in Gros Morne National Park, with views over fjords, coastal lowlands, and old geological formations. The hike to the summit is steep and rocky.

Gros Morne is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, known for its exposed mantle rock and its role in illustrating the theory of plate tectonics.

Robin and I did this hike after visiting Jack in St. John's, who was amazing and let us borrow his car to drive across Newfoundland Island to get to the park.

Signal Hill

August 2, 2023 · St. John's, Newfoundland, Canada

Signal Hill overlooks the city of St. John's, Newfoundland, Canada. The summit rises to about 525 feet (160 meters) above sea level, with views of the Atlantic Ocean and St. John's Harbour. It's known for its role in military history and as the site where Guglielmo Marconi received the first transatlantic wireless signal in 1901.

The hike follows coastal trails past rugged cliffs and the Cabot Tower at the summit, and it's one of the most popular hikes in the region.

I did this hike while my friend Robin and I were visiting another friend, Jack, who lives in St. John's. We ended the hike at Quidi Vidi Brewery.

It's known for its jagged rock formations and the Montserrat Monastery, a pilgrimage site built into the cliffs. The hike has views over Catalonia and passes through rocky spires and forest.

Cinque Terre
